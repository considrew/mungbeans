Most days, the answer is no.

I tested the premise before designing the tool. Buying 0DTE premium is structurally negative-EV across 1,067 sessions — so the system's primary job is refusal, not selection.

The scoring engine is worth building. But it should be built as a gate that stays shut on most sessions, not a signal generator that names a trade every morning. Everything below follows from that.

Finding 01

The base rate

I priced an at-the-money 0DTE option at each session's open using VIX1D as the volatility input, then settled it against that session's actual close. No costs, no slippage — the friendliest possible assumptions.

−15.1%Mean return, ATM put, same

That gap is the volatility risk premium, and it is what the seller collects and the buyer pays. A long-premium 0DTE strategy starts every day in a hole.

Finding 02

Selling before expiry helps — but doesn't rescue it

Your instinct to close the position into a swing rather than hold to settlement is correct, and it is worth a great deal. It is not, on its own, an edge.

I rebuilt the test on 721 sessions of hourly path data, entering at a fixed time and exiting on the first touch of a profit target or stop, otherwise flat by 15:30 ET:

Both sides tested on every session, so direction is unselected — this is the cost of the structure alone, before any view.

Median outcome improves enormously — from −80% to −30% — because you stop donating the last hours of theta. But every configuration is still negative. Exiting early controls the loss; it does not create the gain.

Finding 03

The bar the scorer has to clear

If exit mechanics can't produce edge, direction has to. So I measured exactly how accurate a directional call must be to break even, by weighting each session's winning and losing side:

50%Coin flip → −10.3% per trade
≈56%Break-even accuracy, before costs
60%→ +7.6% per trade
+79.2%Perfect foresight ceiling
The design constraint

Roughly 56% directional accuracy is break-even before commissions and spread. Add realistic friction and the real bar is near 60%. That is a demanding but not absurd target — and critically, it only has to be met on the sessions the system actually trades. A gate that fires on 15% of days and is right 62% of the time on those days is a viable system. One that trades daily at 52% is not.

This reframes the whole build. The scorer isn't ranking which option to buy; it is answering "is today one of the rare days where a directional bet is justified?" and defaulting to no.

Finding 04

The vendor's greeks are unusable

Cboe publishes free delayed chains for SPX, SPY and QQQ including IV and all five greeks. The greeks are wrong in a way that would quietly corrupt every downstream calculation, so I checked them.

At the same strike and expiry, a call and a put must imply the same volatility. Cboe's published figures don't:

Cboe appears to compute IV against the index spot. SPX options price off the forward, and the feed's own index quote is unreliable — it printed a 7675 / 7748 bid-ask around a 7708 level. Repricing the chain naively gave a mean error of $7.01.

Implying the forward from put-call parity instead — it came out 16.17 points above the quoted spot — and solving our own volatilities from mid prices collapses the error:

Validation result

Mean absolute repricing error $7.01 → $1.28. Call and put implied vols at the same strike agree to a median of 0.0001 vol points — an internally arbitrage-free surface. The engine must build its own surface and ignore the vendor greeks entirely.

Finding 05

Instrument choice is not a cost decision

My first pass here was wrong, for a reason worth recording. Measured properly on near-ATM contracts of the front unexpired expiry:

InstrumentMedian spreadAs % of midRead
SPX$0.201.8%Cash-settled, no assignment risk, 60/40 tax treatment
SPY$0.041.7%Tightest proportionally; American-style, so assignment is live
QQQ$0.022.6%Slightly wider; distinct tech-beta behaviour

Near-ATM contracts on the front unexpired expiry, snapshot taken outside regular trading hours — which widens quotes, so these are upper bounds. The build should sample spreads live during RTH and store the real distribution.

All three are close enough that execution cost does not decide the instrument. Against a target of roughly +30% on a winner, a ~2% round trip is tolerable everywhere. SPX earns the default slot on structure instead — cash settlement, no early-assignment risk, and 60/40 tax treatment — with QQQ carried for its different beta rather than being ruled out on spread.

Finding 06

The bug that hides the whole 0DTE surface

Worth stating plainly because it silently corrupted my own first pass and would corrupt anyone else's. SPX options trade under two roots: SPX for the AM-settled standard monthlies, and SPXW for the PM-settled weeklies — and every daily expiry lives under SPXW.

Parsing the OCC symbol by slicing at a fixed offset therefore works on monthlies and quietly mangles every 0DTE contract. My first probe reported zero 0DTE contracts on a chain that in fact carried thousands, and my initial spread figures were measured against the wrong expiry entirely. Nothing errored; the numbers just came out wrong.

Build requirement

Parse contract symbols by pattern, never by offset, and assert that the selected expiry is unexpired. The feed retains settled expiries with stale two-sided quotes, so pricing at T ≤ 0 returns intrinsic values that look like working output. Both checks are in the committed --selftest.

Honest limitation

No free source carries historical 0DTE option quotes. Every backtest number above is model-repriced: real underlying paths and real VIX1D, but theoretical option prices with no historical bid-ask. That is sound for measuring structural drag — which is what I used it for — and not sound for validating a specific entry rule's fill quality. Anything claiming precise historical P&L would be fiction.

The fix is forward, not backward: have the tool snapshot live chains on every run so real out-of-sample data accumulates from day one.

Note on your repo: below-the-line/scripts/options_screener.py reads Yahoo's option endpoint, which now returns Invalid Crumb. That screener is likely broken in production. Migrating it to the Cboe source would fix it — worth flagging separately from this build.

Proposal

What I'd build

A single Python engine plus a static HTML panel, matching the existing repo idiom — pure functions behind a --selftest flag, JSON to assets/data/, prose docstrings explaining the reasoning.

01

Gates — run first, cheap, and fatal

VIX1D inside a usable band; no scheduled macro event in the holding window unless deliberately traded; spread and open-interest floors met; entry inside 10:00–13:00 ET. Any gate failing ends the run with no trade and a stated reason.

02

Direction score — one call or one put

Weighted: trend alignment across 5m/15m/1h and VWAP position (25), momentum thrust (15), volatility regime — is VIX1D cheap against its own 20-day median and against realized (20), term structure VIX1D/VIX9D/VIX (10), 25Δ skew (10), QQQ-vs-SPX confirmation (10), and level structure including net gamma by strike computed from the chain's open interest (10). Below a conviction floor: no trade.

03

Duration — solve it, don't assume it

For the expected move and expected time-to-target, compute return per unit premium net of theta and spread across 0DTE, 1DTE, 2DTE and the weekly. 0DTE wins only when the move is expected fast; a swing needing more than a few hours usually prefers 1–2DTE. The tool reports which and why.

04

Target price — from the surface, not a rule of thumb

Entry limit anchored to mid with a hard cap on how far into the spread to pay. Exit target = the option repriced at the target underlying level, at the expected exit time, with an IV-crush haircut. Stop = repriced at the invalidation level. Output: entry limit, target, stop, breakeven underlying, theta cost per hour, R:R.
